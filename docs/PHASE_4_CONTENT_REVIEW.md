# Phase 4 — First 365 Days content review

## Final scope — 2026-10-31, v1.5.2

The explorer combines 13 bilingual caregiver-reflection chapters with five sourced developmental learning stages. Day/week jumps, month selection, stage shortcuts, previous/next controls, explicit reading progress, collection bookmarking and My Journey integration use existing guest/account storage. No birth date, child ability score, journal or medical observation is collected. No migration is needed.

Reading groups are first weeks–2 months, 3–4, 5–6, 7–9 and 10–12 months. These are editorial navigation groups, **not clinical ranges for acquiring skills**. Each card names its CDC reference age (2, 4, 6, 9 or 12 months). Earlier chapters explicitly say the reader is looking ahead. The content explains the CDC population threshold (at least 75%), individual variation, the limits of selected examples, and the distinction between education and screening. A missing milestone at its reference age, lost skills or any concern prompts contact with a qualified healthcare professional without waiting for another chapter. Prematurity is referred to the care team rather than calculated from private birth information.

UK-specific care pathways are not presented as universal. No feeding, sleep-position, treatment or motor-exercise instructions were introduced.

## Localization and presentation review

English and Macedonian have matching stage/example/activity/source/concern fields. The Macedonian copy preserves reference ages, the population threshold, looking-ahead wording and professional-contact guidance. In-page save states and errors are localized, including account unavailability. Translation is authored and checked for meaning/field parity; no external native-speaker or clinician sign-off is claimed. Whole-app localization remains Phase 7.

Cards use the existing editorial palette and responsive layout. Stage shortcuts identify the active range and move focus to the selected chapter. No added animation is necessary for access. Small-screen, large-text, keyboard, reduced-motion and automated accessibility checks form part of final validation. Reading progress records only explicitly explored chapters; it is never an assessment of development.
